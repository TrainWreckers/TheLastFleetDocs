The horde

So you want to tune the Choir's swarmlings, or teach them something new? This page walks through how they work. The code is in src/LastFleet.AI/Horde, and how it all fits into the simulation is covered in architecture.md.

Trying it

The quickest way to get a feel for them is to throw a few hundred at yourself:

  • --horde 300 calls a horde of 300 when the level starts. With --headless <ticks> it prints how many are alive and how long a tick took. Add --breach <i> to have the horde blow open the station's hull breach i and come through it (add --smoke and --camera to watch one). Use --breach near for the nearest breach it can reach you from, or --breach here to have it come into the room you're in.

  • F3, AI section. First pick where the hordes come from:

    • wherever the horde likes;
    • in my room: on the floor 4 to 10 m from you along the path, all at once, whether you can see them or not;
    • through the nearest breach: the one nearest to you along the path, blown open if it's shut, or your room if none can reach you.

    Then use Horde of 30, Horde of 300, Lancers (a squad of three), Stripper, Spitter and Clear horde (which also clears the Choir's bigger bodies and their acid). The section also shows how many are alive and how many are still coming, and the run's pacing. HordeSystem has its own line in the system timings.

Packs

What happens when a horde shows up while the squad is still sneaking around (the run's hordes before the hack)? It doesn't come straight down the field at you. That horde is a pack.

A pack roams together at a walk (38% of its speed), heading for a spot 8 to 18 m away that it can walk to. The path is found from the spot outward, and each body steps to the next cell closer. Then it picks the next spot, and it gives up on one it hasn't reached within 14 s.

Every body looks and listens a few times a second, and ANY of these sets off the whole pack (Swarmling.Pack; HordeSystem.Packs):

  • someone within 3.5 m;
  • a player's gun fired within 28 m (through walls), or a fight there; a suppressed gun only within 7 m;
  • someone in the open within 16 m ahead of it or to the side;
  • a hit on any of its bodies.

From then on it hunts like any other horde. Start the hack (or the extraction) and every pack comes for the squad! The hack's own hordes, and hordes called by hand, hunt from the start. F3's AI section can call any horde as a roaming pack too (there's a checkbox next to where it comes from).

Tuning

Every number lives in HordeTuning. It's a record, so tests and tools can make their own copy with with.

Setting Default What it does
Speed, SpeedSpread 5.6 m/s, ±12% Running speed. A bit faster than a player runs, and each body gets its own
Acceleration 28 m/s² How quickly they turn and get up to speed
Health 30 Two auto rifle body hits, or one to the head
RoamSpeedScale, RoamMin, RoamMax, RoamGoalSeconds 0.38, 8 m, 18 m, 14 s A roaming pack's walking speed, how far away it picks its next spot, and how long it keeps trying
TouchRange, HearRange, SightRange, LoudSeconds 3.5 m, 28 m, 16 m, 0.6 s What a roaming pack notices: anyone within the first range, a gun fired within the second (within the last LoudSeconds), anyone in the open within the third
SuppressedHearRange 7 m How far a roaming pack hears a suppressed gun (about a room's width)

SoldierCost 10 m Soldiers in a fight count as this much further away than downed players

BreachMin 18 m A breach is only used if it's at least this far from the players along the path (and at most SpawnMax)
BreachClear 6 m Nobody can be this close to where a breach's horde lands
OpenBreachShare 0.7 How often a horde with no breach to blow comes through an open one in reach instead
BreachDelay, BreachInterval 1 s, 0.07 s Time from the blast to the first body coming out, and from one body to the next
BreachPerPlace 40 How many bodies come through one breach before the next place is picked
ClimberShare 0.3 The share that climbs walls and crawls across ceilings
ClimbSpeed, CeilingSpeedScale 3.2 m/s, 0.85 Speed up a wall, and speed along a ceiling as a fraction of running speed
ClimbMinRange 10 m Climbers only go up when their target is at least this far away
DropMin, DropMax 1.8 m, 4.5 m A climber lets go within DropMax of its target, but never closer than DropMin (it drops off to the side)
CeilingMin, CeilingMax 2.4 m, 7.6 m The range of ceiling heights above the floor it can hang from

A couple of gotchas:

  • Hit volumes are constants on Swarmling (Height, Radius, HeadRadius, HeadForward, HeadUp). The model (content/models/swarmling.gltf) is lined up with them, so change both together.
  • Staggering isn't here. 20 damage or more staggers a swarmling for 0.35 s, but that's swarmStaggerDamage and swarmStaggerSeconds in content/defs/loot/impact.json (Studio's Armory > Impact).

Strippers and Spitters

These are the Choir's bigger bodies. ChoirSystem runs them, and every number is in ChoirTuning.

Setting Default What it does
StripperReach, StripperWindUp 2.1 m, 0.65 s A Stripper winds up its blow when it's this close, for this long
StripperDamage, StripperPropDamage 32, 220 The blow's damage to everyone in front of it, and to the props there
StripperCooldown 1.8 s Time between blows
SpitterNear, SpitterFar, SpitterRange 9 m, 22 m, 26 m A Spitter stays between the first two distances, and throws at targets within the third
SpitterWindUp, SpitterCooldown 0.7 s, 3.4 s How long it rears up to throw, and the time between throws
GlobDamage 12 Damage from a glob that hits someone square on
PoolRadius, PoolSeconds, PoolDamage 2.2 m, 6 s, 5 The pool it leaves behind, and the damage it does every half second to anyone standing in it

Their sizes, speeds and health are in ChoirBodies (Simulation), which clients also use to build their copies.

The model

The swarmling is content/models/swarmling.gltf, made and animated in Blender (the guide is creatures.md). Its head and body have to stay where the hit volumes are (see above).

SwarmAnimator picks which animation it plays:

  • StrideMeters (1.1 m) is how far one loop of Run carries it, so the feet keep up with the ground.
  • NearDistance (22 m) is where the far body takes over.
  • DrawDistance (90 m) is where it stops being drawn.

Sounds

swarm_screech (the wind-up), swarm_bite, swarm_death and swarm_skitter (a loop for the crowd near you) are cues in content/defs/sounds.json, just like any other. See sound.md to replace the placeholders.

Over the network

A horde can be hundreds strong, so its updates get their own byte budget per snapshot. SwarmView (on the host, one per client) and SwarmReplica (on the client) send and draw it. How it works is in architecture.md. The numbers to tune are on SwarmView:

  • Range (70 m): which bodies get sent.
  • TargetBytes (360): the client's budget from one snapshot to the next, which the horde's updates are sized to fit. Each update costs about 8 bytes, there are 2 to 16 updates per snapshot, and there are 20 snapshots a second.
  • ExtrapolateSeconds (0.5 s): how far a client keeps moving a body past its last update.
  • The tolerances that decide which bodies get updated first: 6 cm at the client's feet, plus 8 mm for every meter further away.

HordeSoakTests measures these on stations 3 to 10. It checks bandwidth and, on average across the stations, how far from its real position the horde is drawn. That second one is over budget for now. It's a networking task on the roadmap, so the check reports the number and is skipped until it's met. It also checks, on each station, how close each client draws the horde to where the host knows it drew it.
