Surface materials

Every model in the game is drawn with one fixed library of surface materials: painted metal, bare metal, rubber, glass and so on. Your model's colors are still yours. The material just says how those colors react to light: is it paint or polished steel, rough or glossy, how dirty does a station make it, and does it glow?

Why one library for everything? Because then paint looks like paint on every gun, crate and wall, and changing a material (in Studio) changes every model at once. Tweak painted_metal once and the whole game follows!

There are no textures. Faces are flat, and a material is just a few numbers.

Material id What it's for
Plastic plastic Casings, furniture, anything not covered by another material. Anything with no library material gets drawn as this.
Painted metal painted_metal Walls, hulls, crates, a gun's body: metal under paint.
Bare metal bare_metal Barrels, rails, pipes, brass. No diffuse light; its color tints what it reflects. Smooth highlights.
Worn metal worn_metal Floors, deck plates, ducts: paint worn through to the metal, rough and grimy.
Rubber rubber Grips, feet, seals. Always close to black, and rough.
Glass glass Windows, visors, screens. Glossy with smooth highlights, and never grimy.
Cloth cloth Uniforms, sandbags. Fully rough.
Concrete concrete Bunkers, outdoor walls and floors. Rough, and soaks up all the grime.
Emissive emissive Light panels, reticles, signal lights, the Choir's eyes. Its color is the light it gives off.
Sight glass sight_dot, sight_ring, sight_chevron A red dot's or holographic sight's glass: clear except for its reticle (a dot, a ring or a chevron), drawn in the model's color.
Window onto space space_window A window in a ship or station. It shows the sector's sky, whatever its color, and never what's behind it.
Hull plating hull_plating A ship's or station's outside, seen from a window. Lit by the sector's sun instead of a room's lights.

The library is content/defs/materials.json. Each material has these numbers:

  • roughness: 0 is a mirror, 1 is chalk. Low roughness gives a small bright highlight, and high roughness gives a wide dim one.
  • metalness: 0 for paint, plastic, rubber and glass, which light up in their own color. 1 for bare metal, which is dark except for what it reflects, tinted by its color. Somewhere in between for metal with the paint worn through.
  • smooth: its highlights follow a smoothed surface, so a barrel shows one clean streak along its length instead of a stripe on each face. Its lighting stays faceted, like everything else's.
  • grime: how much of a station's dirt it picks up, from 0 (glass) to 1 (floors). Grime darkens the surface, makes it browner and makes it rougher. Anything that moves (characters, the gun in your hands) stays clean.
  • emissive: its color is light. A color of 1 or less is drawn as is, no matter what's lighting it. A color above 1 (a material with an Emission in Blender) is lit normally, and the excess glows and blooms.
  • color: optional. A color it always has, whatever the model says (rubber is close to black).
  • sight: optional. Makes it a sight's glass: dot, ring or chevron. The reticle is drawn where the eye looks along the aim, the way a real one sits at infinity.
  • space: optional. Makes it a window onto space, drawn as the sector's sky (content/defs/skies.json).
  • outside: optional. It's out in space on a hull: lit by the sector's sun, not a room's fill, and seen through no haze.

Reflections

Inside a station, a surface reflects its room: the room's walls, props and lit panels, and what you can see through its doorways. We capture the reflection when the level starts, and again whenever one of the room's lights gets shot out or mended, or the alert stage changes.

Roughness blurs the reflection, so bare metal shows the room sharply while paint only shows a soft sheen. Outdoors, a surface reflects the sky. Characters, flashlight beams and flashes don't show up in reflections; their light shows up in the highlights instead.

Choosing a material in Blender

So you've got a model and want its materials to look right in the game? Here's the flow:

  1. Install tools/blender/lastfleet_materials.py (Edit > Preferences > Add-ons > Install from Disk, then tick Last Fleet Materials), alongside the add-on you model with.
  2. It finds the library on its own when your .blend is in the repository (it looks for content/defs/materials.json in the folders above the file). Otherwise, set Library on the Last Fleet tab of the 3D view's sidebar (N), under Materials.
  3. Pick a library material for each of your materials. You can do it in Material Properties, with the Last Fleet panel's Material dropdown, or for all of the file's materials at once in the sidebar's Materials panel. The choice is saved as the material's custom property lastfleet_material, which every Last Fleet add-on exports into the glTF.
  4. Run Check materials (in the Materials panel, and in each add-on's own Check while Last Fleet Materials is enabled). It lists every material that has no library material and what it falls back to. It also lists any that names a material the library doesn't have. That one's an error, and the export stops.

The kit materials each add-on makes (Kit materials) already have theirs set. A gun's Body is painted metal, its Metal is bare metal, its Grip is rubber and its Reticle is emissive. A room's Floor is worn metal and its Wall is painted metal.

Your material keeps its name and Base Color. The names the game colors itself (Paint_ slots, Body, Glint and the rest) still work, because the library material only controls how the color reacts to light, not what color it is.

A material with no library material falls back based on its name, the same as models made before the library existed: Glass becomes glass, Light (or any material with an Emission) becomes emissive, a Paint_ slot becomes painted metal, and anything else becomes plastic.

Tuning materials in Studio

Studio's Look mode (dotnet run --project src/LastFleet.Studio -- --mode look) lists the library down the left. The selected material is shown on a sphere, a crate and a gun barrel, in front of a row of every other material so you can compare. Pick the lighting at the top:

  • Station lights: a station's ceiling panels, warm white, which is how most of the game is lit;
  • Flashlight in the dark: nothing but the beam from your eye, the hardest lighting in the game;
  • Red alarm and Sodium hall: saturated light, which has to stay its own color;
  • Outdoors: the sun and the sky.

Drag in the room to look around, and use the mouse wheel to bring it closer. model's color sets the color of the shapes. Every change shows up right away, both there and on every model Studio draws (the Armory's guns, a level played from Missions). Ctrl+Z undoes it and Ctrl+S saves materials.json. Add and Duplicate make a new material at the end of the list, and Remove takes one out (plastic stays).

A few rules for the library:

  • An id is lowercase letters, digits and underscores. Never rename one that models use, because they'd fall back by name until they're re-exported.
  • plastic has to be first, because anything with no material is drawn with it. plastic, painted_metal, glass and emissive have to exist, because old models fall back to them.
  • At most 64 materials.
  • Add new materials at the end. Removing or reordering one renumbers the ones after it. The game re-reads models when it starts, but Studio keeps showing models it already loaded with the wrong materials until you restart it.

The library is built into the game, so a change to materials.json shows up in the game after the next build.
