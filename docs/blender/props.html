Modeling props in Blender

Props are the things lying around and standing in the station: the loot on the floor and on desks, the supply cases and footlockers you open, the cargo crates you carry, the cargo pod with its drone, and the station's tanks, reservoir and airlock fittings. Their models are glTF files in content/props/. This page shows you how to edit them in Blender. The game reads the files when it starts, so reshaping a prop doesn't need any code changes!

Every top-level object in every file in content/props/ is a model the game's data can refer to, as file/Object (items/Medkit). A new file, or a new object in a file, shows up in LastFleet Studio's model picker with an icon drawn from it, without any code (the Studio reference). The props in the table below are the ones the game currently draws by name.

The files you start from are the old grey-box props, exported box for box (tools/greybox/generate_props.py made them). Replace them however you like, just don't run that script again over your work, or it'll put the boxes back.

What if a file is missing a prop while you work? The game still draws that prop from its old boxes, so nothing disappears. The station's fixtures are the exception: a station can't be built without them, so keep station.gltf's props in place (the tests tell you which one is missing).

How the game uses them

  • Items stand on their origin, at any yaw.
  • Lids hinge. A lid's origin is its hinge line, and the closed lid extends from it along the game's forward (Blender's +Y). The body's Hinge empty marks where that origin goes. To open a container, the game swings the lid up around its X axis by about 100°.
  • The footlocker's body is built into the level, so it's solid and soldiers walk around it. Heads up: if you change its size, the lid won't fit anymore. Ask a programmer to change the level's footlocker to match (LockerHalfLength and LockerHalfWidth in ModuleReader, and Container.Height).
  • The pod's lid sits at Socket_Lid. The drone comes down onto Clamp in the last 4 s of the defense, then lifts the pod away.
  • The crate is one model used for a crate standing around, held in a teammate's arms, and seen in your own arms in first person.
  • The station's fixtures and furniture have a bit more to them, so they get their own sections below.

The station's fixtures

The station's fixtures (station.gltf) are built into the level's mesh by the generator, not drawn as props.

Where they go. A tank stands where the data's tank kinds put it (Missions > tankKinds: its model, its supplies, and its radius and height, which have to fit the model). The reservoir, its outlet and its valves go where the reservoir goes, and the intake and chute go in the airlock's hull. Each one faces into the room along Blender's +Y. A tank and the reservoir stand on their origin; for the others, the origin is where they meet their wall.

What's solid. They're solid the same way room furniture is: what's solid is set per model in content/props/colliders.json.

  • The shipped fixtures are solid as they're drawn (mesh), so close their meshes, with every face facing out (Studio's Models mode names any holes).
  • Given authored, a mesh named ..._NoCollision is only drawn, one named ..._CollisionOnly is only solid, any other is both, and anything solid has to be a box.

What the markers drive. The game draws a gauge's fill between Gauge and Gauge_Top (0.16 m wide), a valve's wheel turning at Wheel, and the hose from Hose. Supply is colored with whatever the tank holds, and Light is its coupling's glow (dark on a tank that's found empty).

A couple of gotchas:

  • The reservoir's Riser has to be 3.2 m up, where its pipe runs under the ceilings.
  • Several kinds of tank can share the file (Tank_Small). Name each one in a tank kind.
  • Don't regenerate. The starting file came from tools/greybox/generate_station_props.py. Don't run it again over your work.

Furniture

Furniture (furniture.gltf) is stamped into the level's mesh wherever the placement rules put it (Studio's Levels > Props names the model, and Levels > Rules places it). It stands on its origin, facing into the room along Blender's +Y.

What's solid. What it's collided with is set per model in content/props/colliders.json, kept beside the models:

  • furniture is solid as it's drawn (mesh) unless the file says otherwise;
  • a barrel is a cylinder;
  • a model given authored is solid the same way a station fixture is (..._NoCollision, ..._CollisionOnly, solid meshes must be boxes).

Whatever is solid is also what shots hit, so close the drawn mesh with its faces facing out. A piece that breaks (a chair, a glass kiosk) has one solid part (its authored box, or a box or cylinder around it), which disappears when it breaks, along with what's drawn.

Markers. Two kinds of empty mark it:

  • Socket_<Group>_<n> is where loot sits on it: Socket_Top_1 to Socket_Top_4 along a mess table's top, Socket_Shelf2_1 on a rack's second shelf. The data gives each group a loot table, a chance and a count. Keep sockets near the front edge, below 1.5 m and 0.55 m apart, so a player can reach them from the floor and loot on one never overlaps another.
  • Attach_<Group>_<n> is where another piece stands: Attach_Chair_1 is where a chair is tucked under a table. The empty's +Y points back toward the piece (the chair faces the table). The data says what stands there and how far it's pulled out.

The add-on's Socket and Attach buttons add the next one for the Group you typed, at the 3D cursor.

It's forged. Just like the guns, furniture.gltf itself is forged. content/props/forge.json holds each piece's recipe (boxes, bevels, prisms, tubes, rails and vents in the kit's materials, its solid boxes, its markers), and artifacts/bin/LastFleet.WeaponForge/release/LastFleet.WeaponForge content/props writes the file from it, the same way as for guns. Edit a recipe there or in Studio (Levels > Props, Its recipe).

So what about a piece you model by hand? It goes in a file of its own (furniture_hand.gltf), because the forge rewrites ALL of furniture.gltf. The data refers to it as furniture_hand/Sofa.

Directions and scale

In Blender, +Y is the game's forward, +Z is up and +X is right. Units are meters. A prop's origin is the middle of its base, except for a lid (its hinge) and a rotor blade (its hub). Where a prop sits in the file doesn't matter, so we lay the props out side by side to work on. Rotation and scale DO matter, so apply them when you're done (Ctrl + A > Rotation & Scale).

Props you walk around, stand next to or carry have to stay within the sizes the game's rules use. Check reports any that are too big:

Prop At most (X × Y × Z, meters) Why
Items 0.5 × 0.4 × 0.35 They lie on desks and shelves
Crate 0.79 × 0.57 × 0.51 It's carried in both arms and set down against walls (CargoCrate: 0.72 × 0.52 × 0.44)
Pod 1.08 × 1.08 × 1.08 It's set down in front of a player, short of a wall (CargoPod: 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9)
Case 0.6 × 0.4 × 0.3 It sits on a desk
Footlocker and its lid 1.0 × 0.55 × 0.5 It has to fit the level's footlocker

Colors

Most materials keep the Base Color you give them. A few are colored by the game:

Material Color in the game
Glint A warm glow: an item's tag, a closed container's latch, a crate's corner. It's what you spot in a dark room. On a weapon part's case it glows in the part's rarity color.
Rarity A weapon part's rarity color, dimmer than Glint: the band on its case
Supply The color of what a crate holds: fuel orange, water blue, food green, and so on
Light A pod's or beacon's light. Amber while loading, blinking green while its drone is inbound, flashing red while it's being overrun, dull red once it's smashed.
Paint_<Slot> A paint slot. Its color is picked in the game's data for each thing that uses the model (in Studio, a swatch under the model). Its Base Color is the default.
anything else Its own Base Color, darkened on an overrun pod

Paint slots let one model be used for several things in different colors. Let's say you've got a supply case with Paint_Body and Paint_Stripe: it can be both a blue-striped medical case and an orange-striped fuel case!

To add one, select a mesh (or, in Edit Mode, the faces), press Paint slot, and name the slot (Body, Stripe, Trim; letters, digits and underscores, starting with a letter). It adds the material Paint_<Slot> and puts it on the selected faces. Its Base Color is the color something gets if its data doesn't pick one.

The names above are reserved, because the game colors them. Check reports any that are misused:

  • a paint slot named after one of them (Paint_Glint);
  • a gun's game color on a prop (Body, Accent; on a prop they keep their own color);
  • a reserved name with different capitals (glint).

Shading is flat: each face is lit by its own normal. Textures aren't used yet. How each material reacts to light (paint, bare metal, rubber, glass, a glow) is set by its surface material, which you pick with the Last Fleet Materials add-on. The kit materials come with theirs already set (materials.md).

Setup (once)

  1. Install Blender 4.2 or newer.
  2. In Blender, go to Edit > Preferences > Add-ons. Choose Install from Disk, pick tools/blender/lastfleet_props.py, and tick Last Fleet Props.
  3. In the 3D view, press N to open the sidebar. The tools are on the Last Fleet tab, under Props.

Editing a file

So you've got a prop to rework? Here's the flow:

  1. Open it. Start an empty file (delete the default cube, camera and light) and use File > Import > glTF 2.0 to open content/props/<file>.gltf. Save the .blend as content/raw/props/<file>.blend (it goes through Git LFS), so the next person starts from your file.

  2. Kit materials. Press Kit materials. It adds any game-colored materials the file is missing, and shrinks the imported markers. Paint slot adds a paint slot (see above).

  3. Model. Edit the props in Edit mode, or replace them. To replace one, delete it, model a new one, give it the old prop's name and move its origin (Object > Set Origin). Use as many materials as you like. Meshes parented under a prop are part of it.

  4. Markers. To add a Hinge, Socket_Lid, Clamp or Rotor, select the prop it belongs to, put the 3D cursor where it goes (Shift + right-click), and press its button. The add-on parents it to the prop for you. Blender's .001 suffixes don't matter, so the drone's four rotor markers can be Rotor, Rotor.001 and so on.

  5. Check. Press Check. It lists problems in the status bar and the Info editor:

    • two props with the same name;
    • a misnamed material (see above);
    • a missing marker;
    • a prop that's too big, or isn't standing on its origin;
    • a marker that isn't parented to a prop;
    • anything that isn't a mesh or an empty.

    It also warns about props the game draws that the file is missing (the game draws those from boxes). A prop it doesn't recognize is a new model: it has no size limit, and the data refers to it by name.

  6. Export. Set Id to the file name (items, containers, cargo, station, or a new one; it defaults to the .blend file's name) and Folder to content/props/, then press Export. It runs the check first, then writes one self-contained .gltf.

  7. Try it. Run the tests (dotnet test tests/LastFleet.Client.Tests). CI runs them too, so a file that breaks the rules fails the build. PropModelTests checks that every prop is there, the right size, with its markers. Then look at it in the game (below). Commit the .blend and the .gltf together.

Seeing a change in the game

  • The cargo pod and drone: LastFleet --seed 7 --pod 3 starts a station raid with a loaded pod in front of you and its drone 3 s away. --pod 0 gives you a pod that's still loading.
  • Items, cases, footlockers and crates: start any station raid (LastFleet --seed 7). Most of them are in the loot rooms. Hold F (Interact) at a container to see it open.
  • The crate in your arms: press F at a crate.
  • Tanks, the reservoir and the airlock's fittings: start any station raid. The airlock's intake and chute are by its hatch, where you start. Studio's Missions mode shows where a seed puts the tanks and the reservoir.
  • A beacon lying around: drop yours from the inventory (Tab).

The rules

Rule Why
Each top-level object is a prop, named as in the table above, or with any name for a new one. The name says what it is. The data refers to it as file/Object.
A paint slot is Paint_<Slot>. Glint, Rarity, Supply and Light belong to the game. The data colors paint slots, and the game colors the rest.
Containers have a Hinge, the pod has a Socket_Lid and a Clamp, and the drone has a Rotor at each rotor. Lids hinge there, the drone lands there, and the rotors spin there.
Markers are empties parented to their prop. Meshes can be parented too. A prop is the object plus everything under it.
Only meshes and empties. Modifiers are applied on export; armatures, curves and text aren't exported. The game reads triangles and empties.
Crates, pods, containers and items stay within their sizes. The game's rules (carrying, setting down, opening) depend on them.
A station fixture is solid only in boxes, and has its markers. The level's collision is boxes, and the generator places the hose, gauge, valves and couplings at the markers.
Furniture stands on its origin. Collided with as authored (colliders.json), it's solid only in boxes, and a piece that breaks has one solid box. Its drawn mesh is closed and faces out. Sockets and attach points are named Socket_<Group>_<n> and Attach_<Group>_<n>. Sockets are at least 0.55 m apart, and a piece stays at a few hundred triangles (1,100 at most). Level pieces collided with as boxes have to be boxes, and a breakable prop breaks through one solid part. Rays only hit faces from the front. The data uses the group names to give them loot and pieces.
