The graph of places a level's networks can run (LaneKind). That covers a band along every room's walls above the doorway lintels, a higher lane along them (under the ceiling, or up a tall room's wall), a standoff lane offset from the wall to get past whatever already runs along it, straight spans across open ceilings from wall to wall, risers between these and up tall rooms' walls, crossings through walls and floors rooms share (including next to a doorway) and through doorway lintels, and the empty cells enclosed by rooms (service cells).

Remarks

Each lane is a straight edge between two nodes along one of the level's axes. A lane with a fitting in it (ILaneObstacles) is dropped, and a lane along a wall that's blocked by the room's own structure (a hall's rib) becomes a crossing through it.

Nodes and edges are numbered in the order they're found (room by room, story by story, cell by cell, side by side, then the service cells and the floors). That order is the same on every machine, and the router breaks ties using those numbers. No lane crosses a doorway's opening (every lane goes over its lintel or through it, and none goes up a wall where there's a doorway), a hull wall (only walls between two rooms, or between a room and a service cell, are crossed), or the space reserved for standing and props (every horizontal lane is above BandHeight, higher than any prop).
