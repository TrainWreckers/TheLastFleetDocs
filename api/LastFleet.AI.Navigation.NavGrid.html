A layered navigation grid over a level. Each column of the grid holds every floor in it, from the ground up to mezzanines, catwalks and stair landings, so floors stacked over each other are all navigable.

Remarks

A cell is one floor in one column. Cells are numbered column by column, bottom floor first, so a cell number is no longer z * Width + x; use CellX(int), CellZ(int) and Column(int, int).

Sampling: a ray down each column finds the top surface. The ray then restarts just under it and passes out of the solid without hitting anything (faces are one-sided), finds the next surface down, and so on. A surface counts as floor when it's flat enough and an upward ray finds headroom. Below the top surface, that upward ray has to hit the underside of something before it reaches the surface above. If it doesn't, the surface is inside a solid (the floor under a wall or crate) and isn't floor. Every collider is a closed box, so open air always has an underside above it.

Each floor cell links to the floor in each of its eight neighboring columns that's within a step of its height. A cell is walkable when it's floor and every column within the agent's radius has floor reachable by steps, so agents keep their distance from walls and edges. Walkable cells at a ledge also get one-way drop links to walkable floor below, up to MaxDropHeight.

NearestWalkable(Vector3, float)

Returns the walkable cell nearest position within maxRadius meters horizontally, on a floor from 2.5 m below to half a meter above it, or -1. That range means a point in the air finds the floor under it, not the story above or below.

public int NearestWalkable(Vector3 position, float maxRadius = 3)

Remarks

Walkable floor at the point's own height within 1.5 m is preferred. That way someone at the edge of a catwalk (too close to the railing to be walkable) is placed on the catwalk, not on the floor beneath it.
